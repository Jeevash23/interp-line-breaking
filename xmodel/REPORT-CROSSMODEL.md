# D1-X cross-model summary: how seven language models break lines

7 November 2026. This summary brings together the confirmatory results of D1-X, the cross-model part of D1.
- **No new experiment.** Every number below comes from a saved confirmation output (`outputs/xm_conf_<model>_conf-<model>.json`) or, where marked exploratory, from a saved exploration output.
- **Tables:** generated by `code/xm_crossmodel_tables.py`; `--check` verifies that they appear here verbatim.
- **Prose numbers:** checked by `code/xm_crossmodel_prose_check.py`.
- **Per-model detail:** in each `REPORT-<model>.md`.

## What was tested
D1 found that Pythia-410M breaks greedily wrapped lines with four kinds of attention head (`../REPORT.md`):
- **Counters** carry the current line's character count to the word where the decision is made.
- **A stored length** of the previous line is written at the line-start newline token.
- **Token correctors** keep the counters' token-count bias out of the decision.
- **Readers** take the stored length from the newline to the decision.

D1-X asked whether other models do the same, and how. For each model:
1. **Exploration** on WikiText-2 train and on a new-text exploration part picked candidate heads for each role.
2. **Freezing.** A protocol fixed the head sets, thresholds and predictions before any held-out text was read.
3. **Held-out run.** The protocol was run once on held-out English Wikipedia articles created in 2025–26.

Three protocols cover the seven models:
- **`PROTOCOL-X1.md`:** Pythia-1B, Qwen2.5-0.5B and OLMo-2-1B.
- **`PROTOCOL-X3.md`:** Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3-0.6B.
- **`PROTOCOL-X3b.md`:** Gemma-4-E2B, plus its SmolLM2-1.7B addendum.

The tests were the same throughout:
- **C, counters:** share of the count effect the set carries at the decision token (count interchange).
- **T, token correctors:** token weight in the decision after the set is ablated.
- **S or X, stored length:** share of the previous line's effect removed by ablating the set at newline tokens only. S also requires the decision to keep its natural separation. X predicts the opposite: that it does not.
- **R, readers:** share of the stored length's effect removed by freezing the set at the decision token.

Every test was compared with ten random head sets matched layer by layer.

## Summary
- **Every frozen test passed in all seven models: 29 of 29 role tests, and all 7 gates** (AUROC 0.984–0.988 at width 60).
- **Counters carry 59–76%** of the current line's count to the decision.
- **Readers carry 48–113%** of the stored length's effect.
- **Random sets stay small:** at most 9% on any single set, at most 2.3% averaged over a test's ten sets, and a token weight of at most 0.13.
- **The ingredients recur, but their arrangement differs.** In 6 of 7 models a counter head is also a token corrector, and in 6 of 7 a counter also writes the stored length. In 2 of 7 a counter is also a reader.
- **The token correctors are almost all dilution heads.** In exploration, 14 of the 15 named token-corrector heads attend less to the line-start newline as the line fills. In D1's account, this is how a head counts.
- **Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3-0.6B use the same heads, by layer and index, for every role.** The sets were chosen in 1.7B and passed unchanged in 0.6B.

- **Counting and token correction often share a head.** In six models at least one counter is also a token corrector (table above), for example L4H1 in both Qwen3 models, L6H3 in Gemma and L5H21 in SmolLM2. OLMo-2-1B is the exception: its token correctors (L1H10, L0H5, L3H15) carry little of the count themselves. In Pythia-410M, D1 found the counter (L5H6) and the corrector (L5H7) in separate heads.
- **Counting and writing the stored length often share a head,** as D1 found in Pythia-410M. Six models have a counter that is also a newline writer; OLMo-2-1B again is the exception.
- **Counting and reading share a head in two models.** In OLMo-2-1B the count carriers and the width readers are the same three heads. In Gemma-4-E2B one head, L12H3, does both. In SmolLM2 the reader L11H21 also carried the most of the count in exploration (36%), but it was kept out of the frozen counter set, so it is not counted here.
- **Four models have one head in three roles** (counter, token corrector and newline writer): L1H1 in Pythia-1B, L2H1 and L3H1 in Qwen2.5-0.5B, L6H3 in Gemma-4-E2B and L5H21 in SmolLM2-1.7B.

The share is the head's mean attention to the line-start newline; the correlation is with the characters already used on the line. In 14 of the 15 heads, attention to the newline falls as the line fills. In D1's account this dilution is how a head counts characters, and removing such a head lets the raw token count into the decision. The one exception is SmolLM2's L5H21, whose attention to the newline rises with the count; its partner L3H2 is a dilution head.

## Where the models differ
- **How separable the stored length is.** Ablating the newline writers left the decision 85–93% of its separation in Pythia-1B, Qwen2.5-0.5B and SmolLM2-1.7B. It left 70–73% in Gemma-4-E2B and OLMo-2-1B's early writers. In OLMo-2-1B's L5H0 (57%) and the Qwen3 writers (42–52%), the newline state these heads write is needed for the decision beyond the width. The protocols predicted which form each model would show, and each prediction passed.
- **How much the readers carry.** About half in Pythia-1B, Gemma-4-E2B and SmolLM2-1.7B (48–50%), 78% in both Qwen3 models, 96% in Qwen2.5-0.5B, and more than all of it in OLMo-2-1B (113%). In Gemma, exploration suggests where the rest goes. Its later layers reuse keys and values computed by layer 14, so many small late heads can read the newline's state directly.
- **How many heads.** Gemma-4-E2B carries its count with two heads (68%), against three to six in the others. SmolLM2-1.7B reads the stored length with one head of 32 in its layer.
- **Where.** Every named head is in layers 0–14, except three Qwen3 readers (L15H3, L17H6, L22H3).

## Two Qwen3 sizes, one set of heads
Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3-0.6B differ in width (hidden size 2,048 against 1,024) but share 28 layers of 16 query heads. Exploration found the same heads at the same indices in both, for every role.
- `PROTOCOL-X3.md` chose every set in 1.7B and froze them for 0.6B unchanged.
- In 0.6B, C, X and R all passed, which is the protocol's rule for the cross-model claim; T also passed.
- Why the heads match is not tested here; one possible reason is distillation from a shared model.

## What this does not show
- **Scope.** All seven models are small base checkpoints (named 0.5B to 1.7B, plus Gemma-4-E2B), on English Wikipedia prose with greedy wrapping at widths 40–80. Only word ends whose last character is a letter are decision points.
- **Methods.** The tests are mean-ablation and activation patching of attention-head outputs. MLPs carry the count too (D1, and the scans here) but were not assigned roles. Key and value routes were tested only for OLMo-2-1B (`PROTOCOL-X2.md`).
- **Held out from what.** The held-out text was unread by any analysis. Some of it may be in the newer models' training data.
- **Precision.** Gemma-4-E2B and SmolLM2-1.7B have no float32 check, for lack of memory on the local machine. Qwen2.5-0.5B ran in float32; the other four ran in bfloat16 and matched float32 closely in spot checks.
- **No common circuit.** The roles recur, but the heads, their number and how the roles combine all differ by model. The summary does not claim one circuit shared by all seven.
